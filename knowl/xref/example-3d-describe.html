Example 6.10. Describing Equations and Inequalities Geometrically.

Describe the geometrical meaning of the following equalities and inequalities.
  1. \(\displaystyle x = 1\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\displaystyle x = 1,\; y = 2\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\displaystyle x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1\)
    🔗
    🔗
  4. \(\displaystyle 1 \le x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 4,\; z \le 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
  5. \(\displaystyle x^2 + y^2 = 1\)
    🔗
    🔗
  6. \(\displaystyle x^2 + y^2 = 1,\; z = 3\)
    🔗
    🔗
  7. \(\displaystyle x^2 + y^2 = 1,\; -1 \le z \le 1\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
A. This is the equation of the set of all points whose \(x\)-coordinate equals \(1\text{,}\) that is \(\{(x,y,z) \in \R^3 : x = 1\}\text{.}\) Geometrically it is a plane parallel to the \(yz\)-plane, shown in Figure 6.11.
🔗
B. This is the intersection of the two planes \(x = 1\) and \(y = 2\text{,}\) which is a line. The line is parallel to the \(z\)-axis, since \(z\) is unrestricted, as shown in Figure 6.12.
🔗
C. By (6.2) this is a sphere of radius one centered at the origin \((0,0,0)\text{,}\) shown in Figure 6.13.
🔗
D. You can think of \(1 \le x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 4\) as the set of all points whose distance from the origin is at least \(1\) and at most \(2\text{.}\) Therefore it represents the region trapped between the two spheres centered at the origin with radii \(r = 1\) and \(r = 2\text{.}\) Then there is the extra constraint \(z \le 0\text{,}\) which restricts us to the region below the \(xy\)-plane. The result, shown in Figure 6.14, is a half of a thick spherical shell.
🔗
E. In the plane, \(x^2+y^2=1\) is a circle. In space the variable \(z\) is unrestricted, so the equation represents an infinitely long cylinder of radius one whose axis is the \(z\)-axis, as in Figure 6.15.
🔗
F. This is the intersection of the plane \(z = 3\) and the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=1\text{,}\) which is a circle of radius one lying in the plane \(z = 3\) and centered at \((0,0,3)\text{.}\)
🔗
G. The difference between this part and part E is that \(z \in [-1,1]\text{,}\) which means the cylinder is cut down to a piece of height two. Figure 6.16 shows the surfaces of parts F and G together.
