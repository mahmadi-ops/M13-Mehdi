    1. Tangent Planes Parallel to a Given Plane.

    Find the points on the surface
    \begin{equation*} x^2 + y^2 + 3z^2 = 1 \end{equation*}
    at which the tangent plane is parallel to the plane \(-x - 5y - 2z = -3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiplying by \(-1\text{,}\) the given plane is \(x + 5y + 2z = 3\text{,}\) with normal vector \(\mathbf{n} = \langle 1, 5, 2\rangle\text{.}\) The tangent plane to the ellipsoid is parallel to this plane exactly at the points of the ellipsoid that are closest to and farthest from it, so we minimize and maximize the distance to the plane subject to the constraint of lying on the ellipsoid.
    🔗
    Choose a point on the plane, say \(P(3,0,0)\text{.}\) For a point \(S(x,y,z)\) on the ellipsoid, the distance from \(S\) to the plane is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{\overrightarrow{PS}\cdot\mathbf{n}}{\lvert\mathbf{n}\rvert} = \frac{\langle x-3,\, y,\, z\rangle\cdot\langle 1,5,2\rangle} {\lvert\mathbf{n}\rvert}. \end{equation*}
    Since \(\lvert\mathbf{n}\rvert\) is constant, it suffices to optimize the numerator
    \begin{equation*} f(x,y,z) = x - 3 + 5y + 2z \end{equation*}
    subject to the constraint
    \begin{equation*} g(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + 3z^2 - 1 = 0. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The Lagrange condition \(\nabla f = \lambda\, \nabla g\) gives
    \begin{equation*} \langle 1, 5, 2\rangle = \lambda \langle 2x,\, 2y,\, 6z\rangle, \end{equation*}
    so
    \begin{equation*} x = \frac{1}{2\lambda}, \qquad y = \frac{5}{2\lambda}, \qquad z = \frac{1}{3\lambda}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Substituting into the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 + 3z^2 = 1\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} \frac{1}{4\lambda^2} + \frac{25}{4\lambda^2} + \frac{3}{9\lambda^2} \amp= 1\\ \frac{3 + 75 + 4}{12\lambda^2} \amp= 1\\ \frac{1}{\lambda^2} \amp= \frac{12}{82}, \end{align*}
    so that
    \begin{equation*} \frac{1}{\lambda} = \pm 2\sqrt{\frac{3}{82}}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Therefore
    \begin{equation*} x = \pm\sqrt{\frac{3}{82}}, \qquad y = \pm 5\sqrt{\frac{3}{82}}, \qquad z = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{123}}, \end{equation*}
    and the two points are
    \begin{align*} S_1\!\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{82}},\; 5\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{82}},\; \sqrt{\tfrac{2}{123}}\right) \amp \qquad\text{and}\\ S_2\!\left(-\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{82}},\; -5\sqrt{\tfrac{3}{82}},\; -\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{123}}\right). \amp \end{align*}
    The positive signs give \(S_1\text{,}\) the point closest to the plane, and the negative signs give \(S_2\text{,}\) the farthest. At both points the tangent plane is parallel to the plane \(x + 5y + 2z = 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    2. A product on a circle.

    Find the maximum and minimum values of the function \(f(x,y) = 4xy\) subject to the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 = 8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Write the constraint as
    \begin{equation*} g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 8 = 0, \end{equation*}
    so that \(g_x = 2x\) and \(g_y = 2y\text{,}\) while \(f_x = 4y\) and \(f_y = 4x\text{.}\) The Lagrange condition \(\nabla f = \lambda\,\nabla g\) gives
    \begin{equation*} 4y = 2\lambda x, \qquad 4x = 2\lambda y. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solving each equation for \(\lambda\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} \lambda = \frac{2y}{x} = \frac{2x}{y} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x^2 = y^2. \end{equation*}
    Substituting into the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 - 8 = 0\) gives \(2x^2 = 8\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} x = \pm 2, \qquad y = \pm 2. \end{equation*}
    Hence there are four extrema: \((2,2)\text{,}\) \((2,-2)\text{,}\) \((-2,2)\text{,}\) \((-2,-2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Evaluating \(f\) at the four candidates:
    🔗
    \((x,y)\) \(f(x,y)\)
    \((2,2)\) \(16\)
    \((-2,-2)\) \(16\)
    \((2,-2)\) \(-16\)
    \((-2,2)\) \(-16\)
    The maximum value is \(16\text{,}\) attained at \((2,2)\) and \((-2,-2)\text{;}\) the minimum value is \(-16\text{,}\) attained at \((2,-2)\) and \((-2,2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Geometric interpretation. A rectangle with corners \((\pm x, \pm y)\) inscribed in the circle \(x^2+y^2=8\) has area exactly \(4xy = f(x,y)\text{.}\) As the corner \((x,y)\) moves along the circle, we obtain rectangles of different areas.
    🔗
    The values of \((x,y)\) that maximize \(f\) are exactly the values that maximize the area of the inscribed rectangle.

    3. Maximizing a Cobb-Douglas utility.

    A consumer has $600 to spend on two commodities, the first of which costs $20 per unit and the second $30 per unit. Suppose that the utility derived by the consumer from \(x\) units of the first commodity and \(y\) units of the second commodity is given by the Cobb-Douglas utility function
    \begin{equation*} U(x,y) = 10\,x^{0.6}y^{0.4}. \end{equation*}
    How many units of each commodity should the consumer buy to maximize utility?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The budget constraint is \(20x + 30y = 600\text{,}\) so we take
    \begin{equation*} g(x,y) = 20x + 30y - 600 = 0, \end{equation*}
    with \(g_x = 20\) and \(g_y = 30\text{,}\) and maximize \(U(x,y) = 10\,x^{0.6}y^{0.4}\text{,}\) whose partial derivatives are
    \begin{equation*} U_x = 6\,x^{-0.4}y^{0.4}, \qquad U_y = 4\,x^{0.6}y^{-0.6}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The Lagrange condition \(\nabla U = \lambda\,\nabla g\) gives the system
    \begin{equation*} 6\,x^{-0.4}y^{0.4} = 20\lambda, \qquad 4\,x^{0.6}y^{-0.6} = 30\lambda. \end{equation*}
    Dividing the first equation by the second eliminates \(\lambda\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{y}{x} = \frac{2}{3} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y = \frac{4}{9}\,x. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Substituting into the budget constraint,
    \begin{align*} 20x + 30\left(\frac{4}{9}x\right) \amp= 600\\ 20x + \frac{40}{3}x \amp= 600\\ \frac{100}{3}x \amp= 600, \end{align*}
    so \(x = 18\) and \(y = 18\cdot\frac{4}{9} = 8\text{.}\) The consumer should buy \(18\) units of the first commodity and \(8\) units of the second.
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Minimizing the cost of a box.

    A jewelry box is to be constructed of material that costs $1 per square inch for the bottom, $2 per square inch for the sides, and $5 per square inch for the top. If the total volume is to be \(96 \text{ in}^3\text{,}\) what dimensions will minimize the total cost of construction?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(x\) and \(y\) be the dimensions of the base and \(z\) the height. The bottom costs \(1\cdot xy\text{,}\) the top costs \(5\cdot xy\text{,}\) and the four sides cost \(2(2xz) + 2(2yz)\text{,}\) so the total cost of construction is
    \begin{equation*} C(x,y,z) = 6xy + 4xz + 4yz, \end{equation*}
    with partial derivatives
    \begin{equation*} C_x = 6y + 4z, \qquad C_y = 6x + 4z, \qquad C_z = 4x + 4y. \end{equation*}
    The volume requirement gives the constraint
    \begin{equation*} g(x,y,z) = xyz - 96 = 0, \end{equation*}
    with \(g_x = yz\text{,}\) \(g_y = xz\text{,}\) \(g_z = xy\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The Lagrange condition \(\nabla C = \lambda\,\nabla g\) gives the system
    \begin{equation*} 6y + 4z = \lambda yz, \qquad 6x + 4z = \lambda xz, \qquad 4x + 4y = \lambda xy. \end{equation*}
    Multiplying the equations by \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) \(z\) respectively makes each right-hand side equal to \(\lambda xyz\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} 6xy + 4xz = 6xy + 4yz = 4xz + 4yz. \end{equation*}
    The first equality gives \(4xz = 4yz\text{,}\) so \(x = y\text{.}\) The second gives \(6xy = 4xz\text{,}\) so \(z = \frac{3}{2}y\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substituting \(y = x\) and \(z = \frac{3}{2}x\) into the constraint \(xyz = 96\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} (x)(x)\left(\tfrac{3}{2}x\right) = 96 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x^3 = 64, \end{equation*}
    so \(x = 4\text{.}\) The cost is minimized by the dimensions
    \begin{equation*} x = 4, \qquad y = 4, \qquad z = 6. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    1. Why do we need \(y \le 0\) here?
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the extreme values of \(f\) subject to the above constraints.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Else a minimum and maximum would not exist, since we could just pick \(y\) very large and \(x = \pm\sqrt{y + 12}\) would also have a very large magnitude, resulting in a very large or very negative value of \(f(x,y)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. The constraint describes the portion of the parabola \(y = x^2 - 12\) in the third and fourth quadrant. This shape is closed and bounded so we are guaranteed the existence of global maxima and minima. The two boundary points, which occur at \(y = 0\text{,}\) need to be handled separately; this part is not very hard, since we immediately see that \(f\) has value \(0\) on them.
      🔗
      Now we proceed to find the critical points of \(f\) on the parabola \(g(x,y) = x^2 - y - 12 = 0\text{.}\) The system of equations we need to solve is obtained from \(\nabla f(x,y) = \langle y, x\rangle\) and \(\nabla g(x,y) = \langle 2x, -1\rangle\text{.}\) It is
      \begin{align*} y \amp = 2\lambda x\\ x \amp = -\lambda\\ x^2 - y \amp = 12 \end{align*}
      Combining the first two equations we see that \(y = -2x^2\text{,}\) and using the third, \(3x^2 = 12\text{,}\) so \(x = \pm 2\) and \(y = -8\text{.}\) Computing the value of \(f\) at these points, we see that the global maximum of \(f\) is at \((-2, -8)\) with a value of \(16\text{,}\) while the global minimum with value \(-16\) is at \((2, -8)\text{.}\) See Figure 22.75.

    6.

    You have 24 square inches of cardboard and want to build a box in the shape of a rectangular prism. Find the dimensions of the box, if you would like it to have the largest possible volume. See Figure 22.76.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The surface area of the box is
    \begin{equation*} 2xy + 2xz + 2yz = 24, \end{equation*}
    so the constraint is \(g(x,y,z) = xy + xz + yz - 12 = 0\text{,}\) and the function to be maximized is \(f(x,y,z) = xyz\text{.}\) We compute
    \begin{align*} \nabla f \amp = \langle f_x, f_y, f_z\rangle = \langle yz, xz, xy\rangle,\\ \nabla g \amp = \langle y+z, x+z, x+y\rangle. \end{align*}
    The Lagrange system \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) is
    \begin{align*} yz \amp = \lambda(y+z)\\ xz \amp = \lambda(x+z)\\ xy \amp = \lambda(x+y) \end{align*}
    Multiplying the equations by \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) and \(z\) respectively gives
    \begin{equation*} xyz = \lambda x(y+z), \qquad xyz = \lambda y(x+z), \qquad xyz = \lambda z(x+y). \end{equation*}
    Equating the first two, \(x(y+z) = y(x+z)\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(xz = yz\text{,}\) i.e. \(x = y\text{.}\) Equating the last two, \(y(x+z) = z(x+y)\text{,}\) which simplifies to \(xy = xz\text{,}\) i.e. \(y = z\text{.}\)
    🔗
    So \(x = y = z\text{,}\) and substituting into the constraint \(xy + xz + yz - 12 = 0\) gives \(3x^2 = 12\text{,}\) or \(x^2 = 4\text{.}\) Hence
    \begin{equation*} x = y = z = 2 \text{ inches} \end{equation*}
    and these dimensions (a cube) will maximize the volume.

    7.

    Find the point(s) on the surface \(xyz = 1\) closest to the origin.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The distance of a point from the origin is \(\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}\text{,}\) so it suffices to minimize \(f(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2\) with the constraint \(g(x,y,z) = xyz - 1 = 0\text{.}\) The Lagrange system \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) is
    \begin{equation*} \langle 2x, 2y, 2z\rangle = \langle \lambda yz, \lambda xz, \lambda xy\rangle. \end{equation*}
    Multiplying the first equation by \(x\) and using \(xyz = 1\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} 2x = \lambda yz \;\Rightarrow\; 2x^2 = \lambda xyz \;\overset{xyz=1}{\Rightarrow}\; 2x^2 = \lambda \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = \frac{\lambda}{2}, \end{equation*}
    and similarly \(y^2 = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}\) and \(z^2 = \dfrac{\lambda}{2}\text{,}\) hence \(x^2 = y^2 = z^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Substituting into the constraint,
    \begin{equation*} x^2 y^2 z^2 = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x^6 = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 = 1. \end{equation*}
    Since \(xyz = 1\text{,}\) we know that \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) and \(z\) are either all positive or two are negative and the third one is positive. Hence, the closest points are
    \begin{equation*} (1,1,1), \quad (1,-1,-1), \quad (-1,1,-1), \quad (-1,-1,1). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    8.

    Use the method of Lagrange Multipliers to find the maximum and minimum values of \(f(x,y) = \sqrt2\,x - \sqrt2\,y\) subject to the constraint \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With \(g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4 = 0\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f = \sqrt2\,\mathbf i - \sqrt2\,\mathbf j, \qquad \nabla g = (2x)\,\mathbf i + (2y)\,\mathbf j. \end{equation*}
    The system \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) is
    \begin{align*} \sqrt2 \amp = 2\lambda x \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\lambda},\\ -\sqrt2 \amp = 2\lambda y \;\Rightarrow\; y = -\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\lambda}. \end{align*}
    Substituting into the constraint,
    \begin{equation*} \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\lambda}\right)^{2} + \left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt2\,\lambda}\right)^{2} = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{1}{\lambda^2} = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; \lambda = \pm\frac12. \end{equation*}
    For \(\lambda_1 = \frac12\text{:}\) \(x = \sqrt2\text{,}\) \(y = -\sqrt2\text{.}\) For \(\lambda_2 = -\frac12\text{:}\) \(x = -\sqrt2\text{,}\) \(y = \sqrt2\text{.}\) Evaluating \(f\text{,}\)
    \begin{align*} f\left(\sqrt2, -\sqrt2\right) \amp = 2 + 2 = 4 \qquad \text{absolute maximum,}\\ f\left(-\sqrt2, \sqrt2\right) \amp = -2 - 2 = -4 \qquad \text{absolute minimum.} \end{align*}
    See Figure 22.77.

    9.

    Find the point \(P(x,y,z)\) closest to the origin on the plane \(2x + y - z - 5 = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The distance from the origin is \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\text{,}\) so it suffices to minimize the function \(f(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2\) subject to the constraint \(g(x,y,z) = 2x + y - z - 5 = 0\text{.}\) We compute
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f = \langle 2x, 2y, 2z\rangle, \qquad \nabla g = \langle 2, 1, -1\rangle. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The Lagrange system \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) is
    \begin{align*} 2x \amp = 2\lambda \amp \;\Rightarrow\; x \amp = \lambda,\\ 2y \amp = \lambda \amp \;\Rightarrow\; y \amp = \frac{\lambda}{2},\\ 2z \amp = -\lambda \amp \;\Rightarrow\; z \amp = -\frac{\lambda}{2}. \end{align*}
    Substituting into the equation of the plane,
    \begin{equation*} 2\lambda + \frac{\lambda}{2} + \frac{\lambda}{2} - 5 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 3\lambda = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; \lambda = \frac53. \end{equation*}
    Hence the closest point is
    \begin{equation*} P\left(\frac53,\; \frac56,\; -\frac56\right). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    (Only one critical point arises, and the distance certainly has a minimum on the plane but no maximum, so this point must be the minimizer.)

    10.

    Given \(f(x,y) = -x^2 + 4x - y^2 - 4y\text{.}\)
    1. Find the maximum and minimum values of \(f(x,y)\) on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 9\text{,}\) first using Lagrange multipliers, then again by parametrizing the circle.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the maximum and minimum values of \(f(x,y)\) over the disk \(x^2 + y^2 \le 9\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Lagrange multipliers. With \(g(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 9 = 0\text{,}\) the system \(\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g\) reads
      \begin{align*} -2x + 4 \amp = 2\lambda x \amp \;\Rightarrow\; x \amp = \frac{2}{1+\lambda},\\ -2y - 4 \amp = 2\lambda y \amp \;\Rightarrow\; y \amp = \frac{-2}{1+\lambda}. \end{align*}
      Hence \(y = -x\text{,}\) and substituting into the constraint, \(2x^2 = 9\text{,}\) so \(x = \pm\frac{3}{\sqrt2}\) and \(y = \mp\frac{3}{\sqrt2}\text{.}\) Evaluating \(f\) at these two points, and noting that \(-x^2 - y^2 = -9\) on the circle,
      \begin{equation*} f = -9 + 4x - 4y = -9 + 4(x - y) = -9 \pm \frac{24}{\sqrt2}. \end{equation*}
      So the maximum on the circle is \(-9 + \frac{24}{\sqrt2}\) at \(\left(\frac{3}{\sqrt2}, -\frac{3}{\sqrt2}\right)\) and the minimum is \(-9 - \frac{24}{\sqrt2}\) at \(\left(-\frac{3}{\sqrt2}, \frac{3}{\sqrt2}\right)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      By parametrizing. On the circle, \(x = 3\cos t\text{,}\) \(y = 3\sin t\text{,}\) so
      \begin{align*} f \amp = -9\cos^2 t + 12\cos t - 9\sin^2 t - 12\sin t\\ \amp = -9 + 12(\cos t - \sin t) = -9 + 12\sqrt2 \cos\!\left(t + \frac{\pi}{4}\right). \end{align*}
      Since \(\cos\) ranges over \([-1,1]\text{,}\) this ranges over \(-9 \pm 12\sqrt2 = -9 \pm \frac{24}{\sqrt2}\text{,}\) agreeing with the Lagrange computation.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Over the disk we must also check the interior. Setting
      \begin{equation*} f_x = -2x + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2, \qquad f_y = -2y - 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; y = -2, \end{equation*}
      gives the critical point \((2,-2)\text{,}\) which does lie inside the disk since \(4 + 4 = 8 \lt 9\text{.}\) There \(f(2,-2) = -4 + 8 - 4 + 8 = 8\text{.}\) (It is a local maximum: \(f_{xx} = -2 \lt 0\text{,}\) \(f_{yy} = -2\text{,}\) \(f_{xy} = 0\text{,}\) so \(D = 4 \gt 0\text{.}\))
      🔗
      Comparing with the boundary values from part A, the maximum over the disk is \(8\) at \((2,-2)\text{,}\) and the minimum is \(-9 - \frac{24}{\sqrt2} \approx -25.97\) at \(\left(-\frac{3}{\sqrt2}, \frac{3}{\sqrt2}\right)\text{.}\) See Figure 22.78.
      🔗
      🔗

    11.

    Find the minimum value of \(x + y^2 + z^3\) along the part of the plane \(x + y + z = 2\) in the first octant (\(x, y, z \ge 0\)). The solution found by Lagrange multipliers gives the minimum. Use common sense for the maximum value.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Let \(f(x,y,z) = x + y^2 + z^3\) and \(g(x,y,z) = x + y + z - 2 = 0\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} \nabla f = \left\langle 1, 2y, 3z^2\right\rangle, \qquad \nabla g = \langle 1, 1, 1\rangle, \end{equation*}
    and \(\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g\) gives
    \begin{equation*} 1 = \lambda, \qquad 2y = \lambda, \qquad 3z^2 = \lambda. \end{equation*}
    Hence \(\lambda = 1\text{,}\) \(y = \frac12\text{,}\) and \(z = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\) (taking the positive root, since \(z \ge 0\)). From the constraint, \(x = 2 - \frac12 - \frac{1}{\sqrt3} = \frac32 - \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\text{,}\) which is positive, so the point is in the first octant.
    🔗
    The minimum value is therefore
    \begin{align*} f \amp = \left(\frac32 - \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\right) + \frac14 + \frac{1}{3\sqrt3}\\ \amp = \frac74 - \frac{1}{\sqrt3} + \frac{1}{3\sqrt3} = \frac74 - \frac{3}{3\sqrt3} + \frac{1}{3\sqrt3}\\ \amp = \frac74 - \frac{2}{3\sqrt3} = \frac74 - \frac23\sqrt{\frac13}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    For the maximum, note that the region is a triangle with vertices \((2,0,0)\text{,}\) \((0,2,0)\text{,}\) and \((0,0,2)\text{.}\) Since the largest power dominates, the largest value should occur at the vertex where all the “weight” is on \(z\text{.}\) Checking the three vertices,
    \begin{equation*} f(2,0,0) = 2, \qquad f(0,2,0) = 4, \qquad f(0,0,2) = 8, \end{equation*}
    so the maximum value is \(8\text{,}\) attained at \((0,0,2)\text{.}\)

    12.

    You are in charge of erecting a radio telescope on a newly discovered planet. To minimize interference, you want to place it where the magnetic field of the planet is weakest. The planet is spherical, with a radius of 6 units. Based on a coordinate system whose origin is at the center of the planet, the strength of the magnetic field is given by
    \begin{equation*} M(x,y,z) = 6x - y^2 + xz + 60. \end{equation*}
    Where should you locate the radio telescope?
    🔗
    Solution.
    We must minimize \(M\) subject to the constraint that we stay on the surface of the planet, \(g(x,y,z) = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 36\text{.}\) The Lagrange system \(\nabla M = \lambda \nabla g\) is
    \begin{align*} 6 + z \amp = 2\lambda x,\\ -2y \amp = 2\lambda y,\\ x \amp = 2\lambda z. \end{align*}
    The second equation factors as \(2y(\lambda + 1) = 0\text{,}\) so either \(\lambda = -1\) or \(y = 0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Case \(\lambda = -1\text{.}\) The first and third equations become \(6 + z = -2x\) and \(x = -2z\text{.}\) Substituting, \(6 + z = 4z\text{,}\) so \(z = 2\) and \(x = -4\text{.}\) The constraint then gives \(16 + y^2 + 4 = 36\text{,}\) i.e. \(y = \pm 4\text{.}\) At these points
    \begin{equation*} M(-4, \pm 4, 2) = -24 - 16 - 8 + 60 = 12. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Case \(y = 0\text{.}\) From the third equation \(\lambda = \dfrac{x}{2z}\) (with \(z \ne 0\text{;}\) if \(z = 0\) then \(x = 0\) and the first equation gives \(6 = 0\text{,}\) which is impossible). Substituting into the first, \(6 + z = \dfrac{x^2}{z}\text{,}\) i.e. \(x^2 = 6z + z^2\text{.}\) Combined with \(x^2 + z^2 = 36\) this gives
    \begin{equation*} 6z + z^2 = 36 - z^2 \;\Rightarrow\; 2z^2 + 6z - 36 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; (z+6)(z-3) = 0, \end{equation*}
    so \(z = -6\) (giving \(x = 0\text{,}\) with \(M = 60\)) or \(z = 3\) (giving \(x = \pm 3\sqrt3\text{,}\) with \(M = 60 \pm 27\sqrt3 \approx 13.2\) or \(106.8\)).
    🔗
    Comparing all the candidate values \(\{12,\; 60,\; 13.2,\; 106.8\}\text{,}\) the magnetic field is weakest where \(M = 12\text{.}\) So the radio telescope should be located at
    \begin{equation*} (-4, 4, 2) \qquad\text{or}\qquad (-4, -4, 2). \end{equation*}
