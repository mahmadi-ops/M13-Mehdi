    Subsection 2.1.3 Geometric Series

    An important example of infinite series is the geometric series. The geometric series is of the form
    \begin{equation} a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} + \cdots = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} ar^{n-1},\tag{2.1.3} \end{equation}
    where \(a, r\) are real numbers and \(a \ne 0\text{.}\) Note that we can re-write the series as \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} ar^{n}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Let us compute the partial sum \(s_n\) for the geometric series. The partial sum is
    \begin{equation} s_n = a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1}.\tag{2.1.4} \end{equation}
    🔗
    If we multiply equation (2.1.4) by \(r\text{,}\) we get a similar expression.
    \begin{equation} r s_n = ar + ar^2 + ar^3 + \cdots + ar^{n}.\tag{2.1.5} \end{equation}
    🔗
    Then deducting (2.1.5) from (2.1.4) we get
    \begin{align} s_n - r s_n \amp = a - ar^{n}\notag\\ s_n \amp = a\,\frac{1-r^{n}}{1-r}.\tag{2.1.6} \end{align}
    🔗
    Next, to compute the result of the geometric series, we take the limit of the partial sum (2.1.6) as in the previous example.
    \begin{align*} \lim_{n\to\infty} s_n \amp = \lim_{n\to\infty} \frac{a(1-r^{n})}{1-r}\\ \amp = \frac{a}{1-r} - \frac{a}{1-r}\lim_{n\to\infty} r^{n} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Note that if \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) we have \(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty} r^{n} = 0\) and hence \(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty} s_n = \dfrac{a}{1-r}\text{,}\) whereas if \(|r| \gt 1\text{,}\) \(\lim\limits_{n\to\infty} r^{n}\) diverges and therefore the geometric series diverges. That leaves the two cases with \(|r| = 1\text{.}\) When \(r = 1\text{,}\) the partial sum is
    \begin{equation*} s_n = a + a + a + \cdots + a = na, \end{equation*}
    🔗
    limit of which diverges.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 2.1.6. What Happens When \(r = -1\text{?}\)

    We have now handled \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) \(|r| \gt 1\text{,}\) and \(r = 1\text{,}\) but one case is still missing: \(r = -1\text{.}\) Write out the partial sums \(s_1, s_2, s_3, s_4\) of the series \(a - a + a - a + \cdots = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a(-1)^{n-1}\) for \(a \neq 0\text{.}\) Does the sequence of partial sums approach a single number as \(n \to \infty\text{?}\) What does that tell you about the series?
    🔗
    Hint.
    The partial sums do not grow without bound here, the way they do when \(r = 1\text{.}\) Look instead at whether they settle down to one value.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Putting all of the cases together, we can say that if \(|r| \ge 1\text{,}\) the geometric series is divergent.
    🔗

    Summary.

    The geometric series (2.1.3) converges to \(\dfrac{a}{1-r}\) if \(|r| \lt 1\text{,}\) i.e.
    \begin{equation} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} ar^{n-1} = \frac{a}{1-r}, \qquad |r| \lt 1,\tag{2.1.7} \end{equation}
    and diverges if \(|r| \ge 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    Next, as an application of the geometric series, we will go through the following example, which is from our textbook.
    🔗

    Example 2.1.7. A bouncing ball.

    You drop a ball from \(a\) meters above a flat surface. Each time the ball hits the surface after falling a distance \(h\text{,}\) it rebounds a distance \(rh\text{,}\) where \(r\) is positive but less than 1. Find the total distance the ball travels up and down.
    🔗
    Solution.
    As can be seen in Figure 2.1.8, the total vertical distance travelled by the ball can be written as the infinite series
    \begin{equation*} a + 2ar + 2ar^2 + \cdots + 2ar^{n-1} + \cdots \end{equation*}
    🔗
    which can be re-written and then summed with (2.1.7), since \(|r| \lt 1\) here.
    \begin{align*} -a + 2a \amp + 2ar + 2ar^2 + \cdots + 2ar^{n-1} + \cdots\\ \amp = -a + 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} ar^{n-1} = -a + 2\,\frac{a}{1-r}\\ \amp = \frac{-a + ar + 2a}{1-r} = \frac{a(1+r)}{1-r}. \end{align*}
