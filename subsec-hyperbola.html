    Section 12.3 Hyperbola

    A hyperbola is the set of points in a plane the difference of whose distances from two fixed points \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) (the foci) is constant. For \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) the vertices are \((\pm a, 0)\text{,}\) the foci satisfy \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\text{,}\) and the asymptotes are \(y = \pm\dfrac{b}{a}\,x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The formulas for \(a\) arise exactly as they did for the ellipse, with sums replaced by differences. Evaluating the definition at the vertex \((a,0)\text{,}\) the two focal radii are \(c-a\) and \(c+a\text{,}\) so
    \begin{equation*} |r_1 - r_2| = (c+a) - (c-a) = 2a, \end{equation*}
    identifying the constant as \(2a\text{,}\) or \(a = \frac{1}{2}\,|r_1 - r_2|\text{.}\) For the second relation, draw the central box whose half-width is \(a\) and half-height is \(b\text{.}\) Its corner \((a,b)\) lies at distance \(c\) from the center, so the right triangle with legs \(a\) and \(b\) has hypotenuse \(c\text{,}\) and
    \begin{equation*} c^2 = a^2 + b^2 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a^2 = c^2 - b^2. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Notice the contrast with the ellipse: there \(a\) was the hypotenuse, so \(c^2 = a^2 - b^2\text{;}\) here \(a\) is a leg, so \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\text{.}\) That single difference is the source of the sign change between the two formulas.
    🔗

    Example 12.19. Horizontal hyperbola.

    Find the foci and asymptotes of the hyperbola \(9x^2 - 16y^2 = 144\) and sketch its graph.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Dividing by \(144\) gives standard form:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{x^2}{16} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; a = \sqrt{16} = 4,\quad b = \sqrt{9} = 3. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Since the \(x^2\)-term is positive, the hyperbola opens left and right. Then
    \begin{equation*} c^2 = a^2 + b^2 = 16 + 9 = 25 \;\Longrightarrow\; c = 5, \end{equation*}
    so the foci are \((\pm 5, 0)\text{,}\) the vertices are \((\pm 4, 0)\text{,}\) and the asymptotes are \(y = \pm\frac{b}{a}x = \pm\frac{3}{4}x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 12.21. A vertical hyperbola (opens up and down).

    Find the vertices, foci, and asymptotes of \(16y^2 - 9x^2 = 144\) and sketch its graph.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Dividing by \(144\) and being careful with signs,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{y^2}{9} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Now the \(y^2\)-term is the positive one, so the hyperbola opens up and down. In this orientation \(a^2\) is the denominator under \(y^2\text{,}\) giving \(a = 3\) (measured along the \(y\)-axis) and \(b = 4\text{.}\) Still using \(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} c^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 \;\Longrightarrow\; c = 5. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    So the vertices are \((0,\pm 3)\) and the foci are \((0,\pm 5)\text{,}\) both on the \(y\)-axis. For a vertical hyperbola the asymptotes are \(y = \pm\dfrac{a}{b}\,x = \pm\dfrac{3}{4}x\)—note the slope uses \(a/b\) here, not \(b/a\text{.}\) The transverse axis has simply rotated from horizontal to vertical.
