    Subsection 3.3.3 Application in Physics: Work

    Consider the scenario in which a constant force \(\mathbf F\) causes an object to move from point \(P\) to point \(Q\text{,}\) as shown in Figure 3.3.19. The vector \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) is often denoted \(\mathbf D\) and is referred to as the displacement vector. The work done by the force is then
    🔗

    Definition 3.3.17. Work.

    \begin{equation} \text{Work} = \mathbf F \cdot \mathbf D = \|\mathbf D\|\|\mathbf F\|\cos\theta,\tag{3.3.11} \end{equation}
    where \(\left\|\text{proj}_{\mathbf D}\mathbf F\right\| = \|\mathbf F\|\cos\theta\) is the magnitude of the force in the direction of motion, as computed in Subsection 3.3.2.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.3.20. Computing work done by a force.

    A force is given by the vector \(\mathbf F = 8\hat{\imath} - 6\hat{\jmath} + 9\hat{k}\) and moves a particle from the point \(A(0,10,8)\) to the point \(B(6,12,20)\text{.}\) Find the work done.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Consider using (3.3.11), evaluating \(\mathbf F \cdot \mathbf D\) with the component formula (3.3.5). For more help, watch the video below.
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    The displacement vector is \(\mathbf D = \overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 6,2,12\rangle\text{,}\) so by (3.3.11), evaluated with (3.3.5), the work done is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Work} = \mathbf F \cdot \mathbf D = \langle 8,-6,9\rangle \cdot \langle 6,2,12\rangle = 48 - 12 + 108 = 144. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 3.3.22.

    Suppose a mass of \(10\text{ kg}\) is resting on an inclined plane. Gravity exerts a force equivalent to \(mg\) on the object, where \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\) is the gravitational acceleration. Suppose the incline is tilted at a \(30^{\circ}\) angle. Compute the components of the force that are parallel and perpendicular to the inclined plane.
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, we determine the total gravitational force vector \(\overrightarrow{F}\) acting on the mass. Using \(g = 9.8\text{ m/s}^2\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} \|\overrightarrow{F}\| = (10)(9.8) = 98\text{ N} \end{equation*}
    Acting entirely in the negative vertical direction, this gives:
    \begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{F} = \langle 0, -98 \rangle = -98\hat{\jmath} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Next, we define a unit vector \(\hat{v}\) pointing down the surface of the inclined plane. Since the plane is tilted at \(30^{\circ}\) (or \(\frac{\pi}{6}\) radians), a unit vector pointing down the slope is directed at an angle of \(-\frac{\pi}{6}\) relative to the horizontal:
    \begin{equation*} \hat{v} = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\hat{\imath} - \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\hat{\jmath} = \left\langle \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right\rangle \end{equation*}
    where \(\|\hat{v}\| = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The component of the force parallel to the incline, \(\overrightarrow{F}_{\parallel}\text{,}\) is the projection of \(\overrightarrow{F}\) onto \(\hat{v}\text{,}\) given by (3.3.8):
    \begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{F}_{\parallel} = \operatorname{proj}_{\hat{v}}\overrightarrow{F} = \left(\frac{\overrightarrow{F} \cdot \hat{v}}{\|\hat{v}\|^2}\right)\hat{v} = (\overrightarrow{F} \cdot \hat{v})\hat{v} \end{equation*}
    Computing the dot product with (3.3.5):
    \begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{F} \cdot \hat{v} = \langle 0, -98 \rangle \cdot \left\langle \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right\rangle = 0 + 49 = 49 \end{equation*}
    Thus, the parallel force vector is:
    \begin{equation*} \overrightarrow{F}_{\parallel} = 49 \left\langle \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right\rangle \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Since \(\overrightarrow{F} = \overrightarrow{F}_{\parallel} + \overrightarrow{F}_{\perp}\) by (3.3.9), the perpendicular force component is found by subtraction:
    \begin{align*} \overrightarrow{F}_{\perp} \amp = \overrightarrow{F} - \overrightarrow{F}_{\parallel}\\ \amp = \langle 0, -98 \rangle - 49 \left\langle \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right\rangle\\ \amp = 49 \left( \langle 0, -2 \rangle - \left\langle \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{1}{2} \right\rangle \right)\\ \amp = 49 \left\langle -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, -\frac{3}{2} \right\rangle \end{align*}
