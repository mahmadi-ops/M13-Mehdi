    Subsection 3.2.3 Unit Vectors

    Vectors of length one are referred to as unit vectors. The standard unit vectors are the ones pointing in the positive direction of \(x\text{,}\) \(y\text{,}\) and \(z\text{,}\) and they are denoted by \(\hat{\imath}\text{,}\) \(\hat{\jmath}\text{,}\) and \(\hat{k}\) respectively, as in Figure 3.2.15.
    🔗
    Combining (3.2.3) and (3.2.4), any vector \(\mathbf a = \langle a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle\) can be written as a linear combination of \(\hat{\imath}\text{,}\) \(\hat{\jmath}\text{,}\) and \(\hat{k}\) as
    \begin{equation} \mathbf a = \langle a_1,a_2,a_3\rangle = a_1\hat{\imath} + a_2\hat{\jmath} + a_3\hat{k}.\tag{3.2.7} \end{equation}
    🔗
    To find a unit vector pointing in the same direction as a nonzero vector \(\mathbf a\text{,}\) we divide \(\mathbf a\) by its own length. We write \(\hat{a}\text{,}\) read “a hat”, for the resulting unit vector:
    \begin{equation} \hat{a} = \frac{\mathbf a}{\|\mathbf a\|}.\tag{3.2.8} \end{equation}
    By (3.2.5) this vector has length \(\frac{1}{\|\mathbf a\|}\|\mathbf a\| = 1\text{,}\) as required. From now on a hat always marks a vector of length one, as it does for the standard unit vectors \(\hat{\imath}\text{,}\) \(\hat{\jmath}\text{,}\) and \(\hat{k}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Example 3.2.16. Standard Unit Vectors and a Unit Direction.

    Consider the two vectors \(\mathbf v = \langle 1,0,1 \rangle = \hat{\imath} + \hat{k}\) and \(\mathbf u = \langle -1,1,0 \rangle = -\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}\text{.}\)
    1. Express \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v\) in terms of the standard unit vectors.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    A. We have \(\mathbf v = \langle 1,0,1 \rangle\) and \(\mathbf u = \langle -1,1,0 \rangle\text{,}\) and hence, using (3.2.3) and (3.2.7),
    \begin{align*} \mathbf u + \mathbf v \amp= \langle 1,0,1 \rangle + \langle -1,1,0 \rangle = \langle 0,1,1 \rangle\\ \amp= (-\hat{\imath} + \hat{\jmath}) + (\hat{\imath} + \hat{k}) = \hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    B. The length of \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v\) is \(\|\mathbf u + \mathbf v\| = \sqrt{0 + 1 + 1} = \sqrt 2\text{,}\) and hence by (3.2.8) we find the unit vector by dividing \(\mathbf u + \mathbf v\) by its length:
    \begin{equation*} \widehat{\mathbf u + \mathbf v} = \frac{\mathbf u + \mathbf v}{\|\mathbf u + \mathbf v\|} = \frac{1}{\sqrt 2}(\hat{\jmath} + \hat{k}) = \left\langle 0,\; \frac{1}{\sqrt 2},\; \frac{1}{\sqrt 2} \right\rangle. \end{equation*}
